Brantford Black Eye Boxing Hosts ‘Rise Up 2’ to Honour Indigenous Talent

Events The Staff • August 5, 2026 • Views [WPPV-TOTAL-VIEWS] Share

BRANTFORD — High-intensity boxing returns to the community this Saturday, August 8, as the highly anticipated “Rise Up 2” showcase takes over the Brant Curling Club. Hosted by the local Brantford Black Eye Boxing Club, this year’s event carries profound cultural significance, dedicating the entire evening to honouring Six Nations and Indigenous boxers.

Beyond the championship bouts, the event serves as a powerful platform to promote local youth development. Brantford Black Eye Boxing uses the sport to inspire the next generation to embrace a healthy, active lifestyle while having fun. By fostering a welcoming environment, the club provides young athletes with a sense of belonging, character building, and mentorship within their close-knit boxing family.

A massive 14-bout card has been locked in for the night, spotlighting seven Six Nations athletes ready to compete. Local fight fans will see Levon Thomas, Hendrix Thomas, Aiden Hill, Helen Dery, Jasper Pitawanakwat, and Doug Johns step into the ring. Highlighting the night is a thrilling main event featuring three-time Provincial Champion and former National Champion Ava ‘Tuscarora Warrior’ Fenton.

In a unique twist that blends sports with authentic culture, winners and runners-up will receive special, custom-made prizes instead of standard trophies. Local Six Nations artist Mindy Hess has crafted traditional headdresses and dream catchers specifically for the night’s victorious athletes and competitors.

“Rise Up 2” promises to bridge high-stakes sports with vibrant cultural celebration. Organizers have secured a powerful entertainment lineup, including a halftime performance by Lance Logan Keye, who will be bringing four traditional Indigenous dancers for an intermission performance. Setting the stage for the finale, acclaimed artist Layla Staats will perform her powerful song “Rise Up” live in the arena right before the main event walkout.

The doors at the Brant Curling Club open to the public at 5:00 p.m., giving fans plenty of time to find their seats before the first bell officially rings at 6:00 p.m. General admission tickets are priced at $25 and are available for purchase directly at the door. Attendees are reminded that this is a cash-only event for admission. Alongside the action inside the ring, fans can use cash to enjoy a barbecue, shop exclusive event merchandise, and enter the 50/50 draw. Tickets are expected to move quickly as the community rallies to support homegrown Indigenous athletes and experience an unforgettable night of live combat sports.
