Pitt State greenlights 53 million dollar STEM renovation 

by Josie Jones | Sep 28, 2026 | News | 0 comments

Pitt State has announced a fifty-three-million-dollar campaign renovation that will modernize Heckert-Wells and Yates halls. As design work is already underway, the hope is to have the entirety of the project completed by the fall of 2030.  

Chris Childers, Dean of the College of Arts and Sciences, shared what a transformative undertaking this will be for the entirety of the program. 

“Virtually every Pitt State student takes in Heckert-Wells and Yates as part of their general education curriculum,” Childers said. “…So, it touches a lot of students to see the impact a renovated Heckert-Wells and Yates Hall is going to have, not only on the biology, chemistry, and physics department, but on the university.”   

Childers shared that while PSU is state of the art, not all the equipment quite fits that same quality in Heckert-Wells and Yates.  

“We have 21st century faculty and curriculum, but we don’t necessarily have the facilities to match it.”  

The University has lowered their private funding goal to twenty million dollars because of an overwhelming amount of early support. 

“This project is going to be funded through a variety of different sources,” Childers said. “There are federal dollars that have been committed, and we intend to seek additional funding from the federal government; we are also asking for state funding, and looking at grants, as well as private donations.”  

Nancy Booker, chair of the biology department, and acting chair for chemistry and physics, said that the new design and layout will provide more of an opportunity for cross department work. 

“We’re going to increase our collaborative interactions with different departments,” Booker said. “…So, we’ll have faculty for both [departments] on some floors, and it’ll relate to the kind of research they are doing.”  

Booker pointed out the significance of this increased and intentional interaction, by stating that the field of science is better when communication is involved.  

“…People need to understand that science doesn’t happen in a vacuum, it happens when we interact with one another.”  

While this new project is exciting, it will be phased out over the course of 4 years, some students’ entire degree paths. Childers explained that this needs to be met with two attributes.  

“We’re going to need equal measures of excitement and patience,” Childers said. “I think students will be excited when they can state to see the results.” 

Booker noted that faculty focus groups are going to be utilized at every step of the way throughout the process. She stated that these groups will be involved and get to share their visions for the end result.  

“What we are doing is meeting in groups and then I’m taking feedback back to the architects, and the architects are making changes accordingly,” Booker said.  

Booker shared that people coming out of these modernized programs will be ready for their careers and world at large.  

“…People that can come out of here will be prepared for a job market that’s pretty demanding in terms of technical skills, and these people will be prepared to enter that and not have to do a lot of remediation kinds of educational things.”
